# Factor Analysis of Cancer Prevalence in New York City

## Overview
This project performs an end-to-end exploratory factor analysis (EFA) to identify key demographic and socioeconomic factors associated with cancer prevalence in New York City. The goal is to reduce high-dimensional public health data into interpretable latent factors and analyze their relationships with cancer outcomes.

## Motivation
Public health datasets often contain many correlated variables, making it difficult to identify the underlying drivers of disease prevalence. Factor analysis provides a principled way to uncover latent structures within such data and supports more interpretable, data-driven insights for policy and research.

## Data
- Publicly available NYC cancer and demographic datasets
- Variables include demographic, socioeconomic, and health-related indicators
- Data was cleaned, standardized, and validated prior to modeling

## Methodology
1. **Data Cleaning & Preprocessing**
   - Handling missing values
   - Feature scaling and normalization
   - Correlation analysis

2. **Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA)**
   - Determination of the optimal number of factors
   - Factor extraction and rotation
   - Examination of factor loadings

3. **Interpretation & Analysis**
   - Identification of latent factors
   - Mapping factors to real-world demographic and socioeconomic concepts
   - Analysis of how factors relate to cancer prevalence patterns

## Key Findings
- Cancer prevalence in NYC is associated with a small number of latent factors rather than isolated variables.
- Socioeconomic and demographic dimensions cluster together and explain a significant portion of variance.
- Factor-based analysis improves interpretability compared to raw variable-level analysis.
